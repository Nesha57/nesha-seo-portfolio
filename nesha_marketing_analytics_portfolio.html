Case study

Scoopearth: indexing audit and article monitoring

Context: Scoopearth was moving from scoopearth.in to scoopearth.com, so Search Console coverage was changing. My work was to read the Page indexing report, separate expected exclusions from real problems, and check that new articles got indexed.

  • Indexing snapshot (21 Oct 2026): 745 pages indexed and about 10.6k not indexed, across 12 reasons.
  • Largest groups: blocked by robots.txt (3,637), alternate page with proper canonical (3,129) and page with redirect (1,723). Smaller groups: 404 (19), 403 (13) and noindex (12).
  • How I read it: canonical alternates and redirects are normal during a migration. Robots.txt blocks, 404s, 403s and noindex tags are what I verify first and send to the developer when needed.
  • Article check: “Top 10 Laptop Brands in USA” (published 10 Sept 2026). URL Inspection shows the URL is on Google, the page is indexed, served over HTTPS and has valid breadcrumbs.
Scope: these screenshots show indexing status. They do not claim that my changes caused the indexing or any ranking gain.
Featured analytics case study

Outboundry: organic traffic analysis in GA4

Scope: GA4 traffic acquisition and landing page reports for outboundry.com, last 28 days (23 Sept – 20 Oct 2026).

  • Channel mix: 1,289 sessions. Direct 723 (56.1%), Organic Search 466 (36.2%), Organic Social 68, AI Assistant 18 and Referral 11.
  • Engagement comparison: Organic Search had a 47.6% engagement rate versus the 34.6% site average (13.0 percentage points higher). Average engagement time was 34 seconds versus 26 seconds site-wide.
  • Landing-page concentration: the homepage received 624 sessions (48.4% of all sessions), followed by /pricing (63), /rented-linkedin-profiles (22), /lifetime-deal (20) and /mailboundry (17).
  • Interpretation: in this snapshot, Organic Search visitors engaged at a higher rate than the site average, while sessions were concentrated on the homepage. This suggests questions to investigate next, such as landing-page intent and conversion paths; it does not by itself prove that organic traffic converts better.
Measurement note: this is a descriptive 28-day GA4 snapshot (23 Sept–20 Oct 2026). It does not include conversion or revenue evidence, and it does not establish that any marketing change caused the observed results.
